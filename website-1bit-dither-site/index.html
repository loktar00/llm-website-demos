§1HISTORY OF THE HOUSE

    

    

      

        
Adalbert Bayer bought a secondhand platen off a railway
        stationmaster in 1874 and set up in a stable with a leaking roof. The roof
        leaked; the press did not. By the first winter the shop was drier inside
        than out, and the neighborhood caught on.

        
What followed were three generations of printers who argued about ink
        density the way other families argued about religion — loudly, at the
        table, with the linotype running. The rule has never changed: black ink,
        white paper, and the 4 × 4 Bayer matrix, which this house regards as a
        moral instrument as much as a technical one.

        
“Shade is just light and dark arguing. Our job is to make
        the argument legible.”
        — E. BAYER, MASTER PRINTER, 1931

A NOTE BY WAYS OF COLOPHON

    
◆ ◆ ◆

    
This sheet was set in bitmap monospace, ruled by hand, and dithered
    throughout with the 4 × 4 Bayer matrix — sixteen thresholds, two inks,
    zero color, and no mercy for the middle gray. There is no photograph on
    this page; every tone you see is an argument between light and dark,
    settled at the cell level.

    
Printed to your screen at whatever resolution you have chosen, and
    registered in all four corners. Should you find any spot of shade that is
    not a pattern, it is not ours.

Section C — Editorial / Record 006 Extended

The Plant That
Treats Water Like
a Public Room

Most infrastructure apologises. It hides behind berms, paints itself the colour of the horizon, asks to be forgotten. Punta Salina does none of this. The filtration hall sits directly on the Atlantic promenade, its intake channel cut into the same basalt as the sea wall, its exhaust stacks painted the exact orange of the port authority's cranes two kilometres south.

"A building that moves 38,000 cubic metres a day owes the street a legible section — and it posts one."

The architects — a two-person office that has never built anything else this large — insisted the membrane racks be visible through a continuous ribbon of glass at eye level. The utility resisted for four years. The utility lost. Today school groups stand six metres from reverse-osmosis pressure vessels rated to 80 bar, and the floor they stand on is the same terrazzo as the city hall.

Our survey team spent nine days on site. Every dimension in the specification table below was taken with a tape and a laser distometer in February wind. Where the drawings disagreed with the building, we recorded the building.

01 The medium

Overprint everything.

Every risograph print is a small act of weather: layers of ink drift, slip, and settle where they please. Below, three live plates — sun, hills, sea — are being printed in front of you, in the wrong colours, on purpose.

REG 0.0 MM — ALARMINGLY PERFECT

Move your pointer across the plates and the drums slip out of register — just like Tuesday’s cobalt run, which we do not discuss. Let go, and the ink settles where it likes. That readout is our quality control, and it has never once said “perfect”.

  • drag across the plates — feel the drums slip
  • let go — they settle where they please
  • the button pulls one clean proof (rare)

02 Guest studios

Six hands, six drums.

The people feeding the drums this year — each with a studio, a city, and at least one strong opinion about paper.

RISO MZ790

Mara Quist

Studio Pek · Rotterdam

Fluorescent duotones · tide charts for imaginary seas

Runs a two-drum studio above a chandlery. Insists coral ink is a mood, not a colour.

“Registration is a suggestion.”

RISO RV3700

Ossian Beck

Nightbus Press · Glasgow

Cobalt nights · last buses, first trains

Prints the city after midnight from photos taken out of moving windows. All cobalt, always.

“Blue is just black with hope in it.”

RISO MZ1090

Pilar Mendoza

Taller Volta · Mexico City

Cut-paper collisions · scissors you can hear

Cuts every shape by hand before it touches a drum. You can hear the scissors in the final print.

“Scissors first. Always.”

RISO FR3950

Jules Verhoeven

Kopieerhuis · Utrecht

Expired paper · urgent ideas

Rescues discontinued stock by the pallet. If it yellowed in a warehouse for a decade, it’s finally home.

“Paper expires. Ideas don’t.”

RISO SF5030

Ada Nwosu

Second Sun · London

Halftone photography · 53 lpi storms

Turns bus stops and family albums into coarse, glorious dot-fields. Screens are banned in her studio.

“Every photograph wants to be a poster.”

RISO RZ990

Bram de Wit

Statief Studio · Antwerp

Black ink maximalism · slow mixing

Prints “one colour” that takes four hours to mix and three days to dry. Worth it, every single time.

“Black is every colour, overprinted.”

06 Small print, enlarged

Asked at the desk.

A desktop duplicator from the 1980s: it burns your artwork into a master and prints it through a rotating drum of spot-colour ink, one colour per pass. Colours overprint like weather fronts, registration is done by eye and luck, and every copy differs by a hair. That’s not a defect — that’s the whole point.

Yes. Het Drukhuis is step-free throughout, the basement bindery has a platform lift, and we run a quiet hour on Sunday morning (11:00–12:00, drums off). Large-print and high-contrast programmes sit at the front desk — tell us anything else you need and we’ll sort it.

Absolutely. Sunday’s stencil workshop is built for small people (ages 4–104), under-12s enter free with a grown-up, and ear defenders live at the desk because the big press has opinions.

All talks, both all-hands pulls, the yard market, and one guided pull of your own to take home. Workshops are small, so they’re pencilled in separately at the desk — first come, first inked.

Full refunds up to seven days before the fair, no questions beyond “hope it’s nothing serious”. As for rain: the name refers to layers of ink, not the sky. Usually.

Yes — a half table is €15, a full table €25, and 10% goes to the ink fund that pays for visitor workshops. Apply by 1 May; misprints welcome, misers less so.

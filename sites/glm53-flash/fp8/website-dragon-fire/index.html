PLATE I — THE ACCOUNT

The Lore of the Wyrm

I.THE COILING

The shepherds of the Ashfall range do not say that the wyrm sleeps beneath the mountain. They say it sleeps as the mountain — that the ridgeline is its spine, drawn out over centuries and patient as geology; that the caldera is one half-open eye; that on cold nights the whole massif breathes, a slow tide of warm air through the fissures, like the flank of an enormous sleeping animal.

They are careful, always, to say sleeping. The distinction has kept their villages standing for eleven hundred years.

II.THE WAKING

There is an order to it, old as the pact. First the birds go quiet — not scattered, quiet, as if instructed. Then the snow on the south face weeps steam. Then the fissures begin to exhale in rhythm, one breath every nine seconds, and any shepherd counting along had best already be walking downhill. Last, the summit stones shift — not falling, but settling, the way a hound settles before it lifts its head.

Those who have seen the waking speak less of terror than of scale. The head alone, they say, is the size of a chapel. The eyes open like furnace doors. And the wings — the wings arrive last, the way thunder arrives after the flash, unfolding with a creak of leather and cooling rock that can be heard two valleys away.

III.THE KEEPERS

Once a season, the villages carry pine boughs, resin, and a share of the flock to the low shrine at the mountain's foot. This is not tribute, the elders insist. It is companionship. A fire left alone grows hungry; a fire kept company simply burns.

The offering is laid on the black stones, the villagers withdraw to the treeline, and by morning the stones are scorched clean and warm, and the air smells of juniper and struck flint, and the mountain sleeps on.

“We do not feed the fire. We keep it company.” — INSCRIPTION AT THE LOW SHRINE, C. 812

PLATE III — FIELD DOCTRINE

The Breath

THE SUBJECT IS ASLEEP. TREAD SOFTLY.

The wyrm does not breathe fire. It makes it — briefly, chemically, and with evident relish. Deep in the chest sits a second heart: not a pump of blood but a gland of two chambers, one yielding a volatile thorite oil, the other a catalyst so eager that combustion begins the instant the two meet at the back of the throat.

Contraction of the thoracic bellows drives the mixture forward at tremendous pressure. Ignition occurs at the teeth — sparkless, instantaneous, and accompanied by a low chord, like a cathedral organ heard through a stone wall. What leaves the mouth is less a flame than a directed weather event.

Kindling Point

Surveys agree the plume self-ignites within a wingspan of the jaw. Beyond four spans it behaves like a river of light: it bends around stone, pools in hollows, and reads handwriting. Survivors describe the heat as respectful — total, but impersonal, like an audit.

Afterglow

When the charge is spent the jaw closes with a click of cooling enamel and the embers drift downward for minutes afterward, settling into the margins of the valley like coins into a purse. The smoke smells of juniper and struck flint. The silence afterward is described, uniformly, as polite.

Snow falls grey for a season. Not one head of livestock is lost. The villages take this, correctly, as a compliment.

THE LONDON QUESTION

Chroniclers blame the wyrm for the Great Fire. A baker's boy confesses. Through intermediaries, the wyrm declines comment.

1902

THE PELÉE CONFUSION

A naturalist attributes the catastrophe on Martinique to "our subject, abroad." He retracts; the wyrm has never left the range.

1976

THE HARRIER INCIDENT

An RAF pilot shadows "a wing-shaped radar echo, ninety knots, on no fuel." The report is filed under weather.

2011

ANOMALY KV-9

A satellite registers a thermal hotspot that climbs uphill against the weather, pauses, and looks — the analysts insist — back.

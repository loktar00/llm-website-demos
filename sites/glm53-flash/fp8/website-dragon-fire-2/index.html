II · Lore

The Waking

Being the accepted account of the Cinder Reach, together with certain marginal disputes.

Long before the first map gave the mountain a name, it had named itself, in the only language it speaks: heat. The miners of the Cinder Reach learned to read it the way sailors read weather. A warm wind from the north meant the Wyrm had turned over in its sleep. A cold one meant it was listening.

They did not hunt it, and they did not worship it — except in the ways that are none of your business. What the valley did was strike a bargain, unwritten and unsigned: a basin of brine left at the treeline on every full moon, and in exchange the mountain would keep its breath to itself. The basins are still there. The brine is still gone by morning. No surveyor has ever waited long enough to see who collects it, and the Guild respects that kind of honesty.

For a hoard, it keeps no gold. It keeps warmth, which is a worse thing to owe. Old men swear the winters have been gentle since the pact; that the snow never settles past the second ridge; that their grandfathers died warm in beds above frozen ground. Whether this counts as mercy or as interest on a loan, the Chronicle declines to say.

“It does not sleep. It waits — at a temperature.”

— carved above the door of Firewatch Station One, author unknown, door also unknown

What is known is this: roughly once a century, the Wyrm wakes. The birds go quiet in a spreading line, like a curtain being drawn. The rock gains a pulse. And the creature that has been coiled around the valley's whole history — patient as geology, vain as fire — lifts its head to see what the century has brought. This time, it brought you.

Margin note · disputed

The Wyrm has been measured at sixty-one paces from snout to tail-fork, though never twice by the same surveyor, and never by the same surveyor twice.

IV · On the Flame

Ignis Visceris

On the combustion of the second heart. Transcribed by the only Guild member who agreed to stand downwind.

The Wyrm does not make fire the way a lamp makes light — casually, and everywhere. It keeps two glands where lesser creatures keep one heart. The anterior alembic distils an aerosol of pyroferous vapour from everything the mountain feeds it: sulphur, iron-spring water, and the accumulated grudges of a very long life. The posterior sac holds the catalyst, which the chemists' register describes only as “unctuous, and opposed to being catalogued.”

At the moment of exhalation the two meet in the tracheal vault and ignite against the palatal spark-ridge — a mechanism uncomfortably similar to striking a match against the inside of your own teeth. The plume leaves the mouth at the speed of a regret, at a temperature at which stone remembers it used to be lava. What you see sweeping this page is a courtesy: roughly one-tenth of a full breath, adjusted for audience.

Note the residue. The ash it leaves behind is famously fine, faintly warm, and — the bakers of Hollowford insist — unmatched for crust. The Guild neither confirms nor denies the existence of the Wyrm's sourdough.

Propellant
Aerosolised pyroferous distil, specific gravity 0.71, smells of struck flint and holidays
Ignition
Palatal spark-ridge; four thousand strikes per warming, self-sharpening
Plume
1,430 °C at three rods; nine rods level; further downhill, as gossip does
Sustain
Nine full breaths, then a rest that the wise do not interrupt
Residue
Ash, faintly warm, useful for bread and for apologies
Live reading · Guild of Cautious Men
12 °C

Instrument: iron rod, held at a prudent distance, reported honestly. The figure rises with the Wyrm's actual breath on this page.

Field observation

The flame does not burn so much as insist. Timber does not catch; it concedes.

V · Sightings

A Chronicle of Encounters

Seven hundred years of witnesses, in ascending order of credibility.

  1. c. 1100

    The Salt Pact

    The basin tradition begins. Nobody signs; everybody agrees. The brine is gone by morning, as it has been every morning since.

  2. 1287

    The Sneeze of Hollowford

    The abbey chronicle blames lightning. The lightning, witnesses maintained, smelled of sulphur and appeared to have opinions about the bells.

  3. 1491

    The Cartographer's Correction

    The mountain is re-measured eleven rods further east than any map allowed. The maps are burned for insolence. The mountain declines to comment.

  4. 1734

    The Lights of Ash Vale

    Ninety consecutive nights of orange over the ridge. The parish register records simply: “weather.” Two baptisms are postponed; both children are named Ember.

  5. 1889

    The Dirigible Perpetua

    Rose too high, came down warm. The crew reported that the sky “had a heartbeat,” and that none of them wished to discuss it further, at length, repeatedly, for the rest of their lives.

  6. 1977

    The Firewatch Photographs

    One photograph. Seven committee resignations. The negative is kept in a cold room, as it does not develop the same way twice, and one of the ways was rude.

  7. Now

    This Account

    You have just become the latest sighting on record. It watched you read. It is not finished watching.

Explicit Liber Septimus

Here ends the account of Vyrenax, last wyrm of the Cinder Throne — who has never once hoarded gold, and has always, regrettably, hoarded warmth. Should you feel unusually comfortable this winter, you will know the basin was full.

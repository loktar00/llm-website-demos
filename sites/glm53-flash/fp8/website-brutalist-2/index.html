HAVSVÄRKET COASTAL TREATMENT WORKS — SIX YEARS OF POURING, ONE SENTENCE OF ARCHITECTURE: NOTHING HERE IS DECORATED, AND NOTHING HERE IS DECIDED TWICE.

CASE N°

KLY-2025-0117

Approach Havsvärket from the water and it does not look like architecture. It looks like intent — a 618-metre finger of board-marked concrete pushed into the Klyde Sound, its deck held two metres above the highest recorded tide, as if the sea had been served a written warning. There is no cladding because there is nothing to hide. The formwork was built by carpenters who signed each panel in pencil, and the signatures are still legible sixty metres offshore, where nobody was ever expected to read them.

The plan is a sentence: screens, pumps, air, settlement, contact, sea. Four caissons of 2,600 tonnes each were sunk into the seabed in the spring of 1976, and the sinking took eleven days because the crew refused to rush a pour. Two raw-water galleries fall toward the diffusers at a gradient so slight that the survey team argued about it for a month, then engraved the number into the gallery wall, where it remains. The diffusion heads sit a kilometre offshore in eleven metres of water; the plant's own engineers call the rising plume "the weather."

"NOTHING HERE IS DECORATED. EVERYTHING HERE IS DECIDED."

— SITE NOTICE, PUMP ROOM DOOR. PAINTED 1979. REPAINTED ELEVEN TIMES. NEVER REWORDED.

Inside, the pump room is a basilica. Nine line-shaft pumps stand in a colonnade beneath a clerestory of north-facing glass; the floor is painted the orange of the plant's own safety code, the only colour on the entire site. The room's acoustics are such that a dropped spanner files a report to every corner of the building at once. Chief engineer Brigitte Halloran called it "the argument-settler." She was right, and the minutes of every meeting held there agree with her.

This spring the municipality filed for partial demolition of the contact tanks, to make room for a "flexible waterfront platform." The archive's position is printed beneath the drawings, as always: redraw it, measure it, publish it, refuse to lose it. Sheet S-101 below carries the numbers. The numbers are not sentimental. That is precisely why they are dangerous.

02/ ISSUE 042 — FEATURED DISPATCH

      

        
Death of the Dial-Up — a eulogy, typed at 2400 baud

        
by GHOST-09 · 14 pages · best read with the lights off and a cup of something hot


        
It started as a sound. The modem handshake — that screaming, chirping,
        electric bird-song that meant a machine on the other end of a copper wire
        was, for a few minutes, here. We called it a connection. We meant
        it the way we meant every other word in this trade: literally, and
        spiritually, and with a little fear attached.


        
    .-----------------------------.
    |  MODEL: 2400-BD PSALM-89    |
    |  [TONE] [SYNC] [CARRIER OK] |
    '-----------------------------'
       |        |        |
       |        |        |
   PHONE JACK  POWER   "THE SCREAM"
       |
       |  ~~~~~~   2400 baud  ~~~~~~
       |    /\   /\   /\   /\
       |   /  \ /  \ /  \ /  \
       v   SIGNAL TO THE MACHINE SPIRITS

        
fig.1 — a residential modem, mid-handshake. the waveform is accurate. the spirits are not.


        
What do you do when the birds stop singing? This issue is my answer.
        I rode the line from a bedroom in 1989, shared it with two siblings and a
        parent who believed "the internet" was what they did at the office with
        the big calculator, and I watched it grow from a rumor into a
        continent. The tools got faster. The rooms got colder. The ghosts
        learned to render at 60 frames per second and stopped knocking.


        

          ghost-09@bedroom:~$ who am i

          you are a packet that got nowhere, and therefore, all over.
        


        
The practical advice, for those still dialing: learn your handshakes
        by ear. Know the difference between a carrier tone and a busy tone the
        way a shepherd knows his sheep. Learn AT&D0 and
        ATS0=1 by heart. Keep a hand-written log of
        bulletin boards in a notebook that never touches a scanner. When the
        line drops at 3:00 a.m. and the modem emits its final descending
        wail — the sound of a connection dying — write it down.
        Someday the only proof we were ever logged in will be our notes.


        
              +-----------------------+
              |  CARRIER LOST        |
              |  AT&E0Q0             |
              |  [no tone]           |
              |  [no tone]           |
              |  [no ton            |
              |  [no t             |
              |  [                |
              |   [              |
              |    [            |
              |     [  the light |
              |      [  is warm  |
              |       [  on its  |
              |        [  way out |
              |         [        |
              +-------------------

        
fig.2 — carrier lost. the phosphor holds the last characters a moment longer than the power does.


        
This zine will not pretend otherwise: something ended in 1997, and
        it has been ending ever since, at 2400 baud, a little bit every night,
        every time the line goes dead and we reach for the handset out of
        muscle memory instead of grief. But the null device still works.
        Nothing goes in, nothing comes out, and it is always, always open.
        That is the whole philosophy. See you in issue 043.

        
— end of transmission — press [RETURN] to scroll to the crew.

NAME

          
nullzine — an underground hacker culture zine, delivered in phosphor, printed nowhere.


          
SYNOPSIS

          
nullzine [--issue N] [--ink green] [--baud 2400] [--lights off]


          
AUTHORS

          

            GHOST-09 (a.k.a. The Switchboard Ghost) — editor-in-chief. former telephone exchange
            technician, 1987–1991. claims the modem in fig.1 is haunted;
            will not elaborate, will hum along with it.
            

            NULLPTR (a.k.a. The Pointer) — layout and box-drawing. types everything in lowercase
            except the words ALWAYS and OPEN. keeps the
            ASCII art honest to 80 columns, one column at a time.
            

            BAUD-48 (a.k.a. Slow Signal) — sound archive. recorded 312 modem handshakes on
            cassette; the cassettes are the issue, the cassettes are the
            whole issue.
            

            ROOTKIT (a.k.a. The Privilege) — dispatches and interviews. only reachable during
            the day. publishes under a name she has never, so far, used
            anywhere else.
            

            /dev/null (a.k.a. The Device) — final proofreader. accepts all submissions, retains
            none. considered it a compliment when nobody could be reached
            for corrections.

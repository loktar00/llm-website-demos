The Printers

Mara Okafor

Marrow Print Collective

Runs a two-drum riso on a farm trailer. Famous for enormous coral skies and a strict rule: every plate gets one happy accident.

Devan Marsh

Weather & Ink Ltd.

Prints the actual weather every morning: pressure maps, gust charts and one barometer that has never once been wrong.

Aiko Tanaka

Overprint Studio

Believes every duotone hides a third colour. Prints in the dark, by feel, and swears the drum hums in D minor.

Sam Okonkwo

Drift Press

Professional misregistrator. His posters deliberately walk apart under magnifying glasses. Keeps a wall of 'wrong' prints, called the good wall.

Ines Ferreira

Slow Roll

Prints one poster a month, on purpose. Her editions sell out in hours and her drying rack is, in her words, load-bearing infrastructure.

Noor & Ben

Paper Storm

A two-person studio in a converted launderette. If a paper has a funny fold, they print on it. Their stockist map has no straight lines.

Questions

What is a risograph?

A riso (or risograph) is a hybrid print machine that stamps each colour one at a time onto recycled-ish paper, like a very fast, very inky photocopy of your art. Each colour is its own 'plate' — which is why you can see layers, overprints and the occasional glorious misregistration. It's loud, a little smelly (in the best way) and slightly magic.

Do I need experience to join a workshop?

No. Every workshop is beginner-friendly unless it says otherwise, and each one is capped at 14 people so you'll actually touch the machine. Wear clothes you don't mind smudging — we provide aprons, but the ink has opinions.

Are tickets and posters available online?

Festival tickets sell online and at the door (door price is a little higher, like everywhere). Posters from the shop here sell online before the fair and at every studio table on the day — editions are numbered and truly limited. When it says sold out, the drum has stopped.

Is the venue accessible?

Yes. The Old Foundry has step-free access on the cobalt-marked route from Foundry Lane, accessible toilets in the hall, a quiet room by the ink bar, and British Sign Language interpretation at all main-hall talks. Tell us at booking time if there's anything we should prepare.

Can I print my own work at the fair?

Yes! Bring 300dpi PDFs (or originals, we'll plate them) and queue at the guest print desk in the hall. Small runs — 5 sheets, 4 colours — about 20 minutes each, £6 per sheet. Come early; the queue is a social event in itself.

What about the weather? Obviously I ask.

The parade happens in any weather, and the weather is the whole point. There is cover on the hall side if it's truly dreadful, and the mint team issues free ponchos. We print what the sky does; we just get paid in compliments.

01 — THE STRUCTURE

Pill Rock is five rooms of board-marked concrete holding back the Atlantic's appetite for fresh water. The primary filtration hall spans 34 metres without a column. The ceiling still carries the timber grain of the 1979 formwork — an accidental fresco of every plank the crew ever nailed.

Nothing was added that could be justified by maintenance manuals. Nothing was removed that carried load.

02 — THE THREAT

Two demotion orders name the plant "operationally redundant." Both were filed within eleven days of a coastal land-bidding notice. The archive does not draw conclusions. The archive files the dates side by side and lets the reader bring the hammer.

03 — THE RECORD

We hold 14 pages: original tender sheets, two survey passes, one sealed letter from the engineer of record, and 312 photographs taken across four seasons of storm access. The file is complete. The file is copyable. The file will outlive every office that issued a demolition permit against it.

§04 — POSITION
FRAGMENTED BY DESIGN / READ IN ANY ORDER / ORDER CHANGEABLE
01

The building does not explain itself. It stands. Our job is the opposite of PR: describe, measure, shut up, file.

02

Every demolished civic structure takes the memory of its use with it. We keep the receipt and the building's side of the story.

03

No renderings. No filters. No drone shots of places we weren't allowed to enter on foot.

04

Heritage listing is a committee. An archive is a witness. We have found committees to be slower and less honest.

FILED · NOT FORGIVEN
05

Concrete is a record medium. The formwork remembers the carpenter. We merely transcribe.

06

Accept no sponsorship from any party that has ever filed a demolition notice. That is most parties. So: none. Funded by 2,109 members and absolutely no one else.

07

A city that names its towers after debt and its water plants after no one at all has a memory problem, not a housing problem.

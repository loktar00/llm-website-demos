Pages from the field

A few things I stopped to notice.

12 April · Spring

The slow art
of waking up

Every fern is a small green fist, loosening its grip on winter.

Read field note

The shaded bank is a week behind the meadow. I marked three curled fronds with fallen twigs; by next Sunday, each had opened another pair of leaflets. Growth has its own unhurried clock.

06 July · Summer

A blue flash
above the burn

Ten minutes beside the water. A whole afternoon, if you ask the damselflies.

Read field note

In the sheltered bend, bright blue damselflies returned to the same grass stems again and again. One held its perch through three passing shadows before lifting into the sun.

03 December · Winter

What the
quiet leaves

A feather on the path. The bird has gone, but not entirely.

Read field note

Frost edged the feather without weighing it down. I sketched it where it lay, then listened. Somewhere beyond the hazel, a wren broke the silence with a voice much larger than its body.

04 / A LONGER LOOK
From the autumn pages

The tree that
didn't stop giving

18 OCTOBER 2025   ·   THE FALLEN BEECH   ·   4 MINUTES OF QUIET

For three years I walked past the fallen beech and thought of it as a tree that had died. Today I sat beside it long enough to realise how much that description leaves out.

The bark has loosened into small, curling doors. Under one, a woodlouse pauses with the solemn patience of an animal that has no interest in my notebook. There are pale threads in the wood, the quiet work of fungi. What once stood tall is being returned, fibre by fibre, to the forest.

Perhaps a fallen tree is simply
a forest at a different height.

A cluster of mushrooms has appeared along the upper edge since last week's rain. Each cap holds a bead of water. Moss fills the old branch scars so thickly that they look upholstered. A wren slips through a gap and vanishes somewhere inside the trunk.

I had come to draw mushrooms. Instead I spend an hour drawing the spaces between things: the hollow under a root, the dark line beneath a strip of bark, the small green pocket where an acorn has lodged.

By the time I leave, the page is full and the tree is still busy. I write one last thing in the margin: come back. There is more.

↳ Observation, not conclusion.

07 / A NOTE FROM THE NATURALIST

Hello, I'm Rowan.

Walker, watcher, collector of small details. I started this notebook to learn the names of things. Somewhere along the way, it taught me how to pay attention.

These are my notes from one ordinary patch of Northumberland. The drawings aren't perfect. Neither are the identifications. But every page begins the same way: by stopping to look.

Rowan

Editorial Feature

The Atlantic
Coastal
Water Plant

Rising from the Tagus estuary like a monolith of poured决心, the Atlantic Coastal Water Plant represents the culmination of Portugal's post-1974 civic infrastructure renaissance. Designed by the Lisbon-based firm Estúdio Concreto, the facility processes 120 million liters of Atlantic seawater daily.

Water Treatment Coastal Infrastructure Brutalist Revival Estúdio Concreto CI-006

The Atlantic Coastal Water Plant does not ask for permission. It announces itself across the Tagus estuary with the unapologetic mass of 85,000 square meters of fiber-reinforced concrete, a structure that processes 120 million liters of seawater each day and delivers it to the Lisbon metropolitan area's two million residents.

Designed by Estúdio Concreto — a collective founded in 1998 by architects Maria Vasconcelos and João Pereira — the plant is the largest single civic construction project in Portugal since the completion of the Vasco da Gama Bridge. Its angular geometry references the fortified coastal batteries that once defended the estuary, while its internal logic is entirely contemporary.

"We do not build for posterity. We build for the water that must flow today, the people who must drink tomorrow."
— Maria Vasconcelos, Estúdio Concreto, 2001

The facility's treatment process spans four sequential chambers, each progressively larger and lower, following the natural gradient of the site toward the river. Raw seawater enters through a coastal intake structure built into the cliff face, passes through sand filtration beds, reverse osmosis membranes, and mineralization tanks before emerging as potable water in the distribution hall.

What distinguishes the plant from similar facilities worldwide is its deliberate exposure of infrastructure as architecture. The concrete shell is left raw — no cladding, no paint, no attempt to soften the brutalist aesthetic. Structural ribs, pipe conduits, and ventilation shafts are visible from the exterior, celebrated rather than concealed.

STATEMENT OF INTENT
CONCRETE INDEX FOUNDERS
REVISED 2024
I.

Infrastructure Is Monumental

The buildings that serve the public — water plants, transit stations, sewage treatment facilities, electrical substations — are the true monuments of our age. They are built to last centuries, they move millions of people, they clean the water we drink. They deserve documentation, celebration, and study.

II.

Against the Invisible

Civic architecture is designed to be ignored. We refuse this erasure. Every poured wall, every precast element, every exposed conduit is a statement of public intent.

III.

Material Honesty

Concrete does not lie. Steel does not pretend. Glass does not disguise. We document buildings that refuse the artifice of cladding and facade. The structure is the architecture. Nothing more, nothing less.

IV.

The Archive as Public Work

This index is itself a civic structure — a repository of collective knowledge about the built environment.

V.

Against Obsolescence

We catalog structures that are already aging, already weathering, already showing the marks of time and use. These are not flaws — they are records of service. A building that has worked for fifty years has earned its documentation. We build this archive so that when these structures are demolished, they are not forgotten.

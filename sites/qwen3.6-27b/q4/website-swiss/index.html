04 — Timeline

Information Design

Key moments in the evolution of transit wayfinding and public information systems.

1933
Harry Beck's London Underground Map
The schematic revolution: topological accuracy replaces geographical fidelity. A circuit diagram for the city.
1954
Max Bill's Zurich Street Signs
Geometric reduction of directional signage to essential forms. The Swiss style enters the public realm.
1962
Otl Aicher & the Frankfurt Airport System
A unified pictogram language for international wayfinding, establishing standards still in use globally.
1972
Massimo Vignelli's New York Subway Map
The most iconic transit map ever produced. Pure geometry, primary colors, total clarity. Withdrawn in 1980 after public outcry over omitted stations.
1985
SBB Corporate Identity Expansion
The Swiss Federal Railways extends its graphic system across all passenger information touchpoints.
2001
Digital Departure Boards Go Real-Time
LCD displays replace mechanical flip-dot systems. Information becomes dynamic, predictive, networked.
2018
Accessible Multimodal Navigation
Wayfinding integrates audio, haptic, and visual channels. The information system becomes inclusive by design.

Featured Essay

On the Geometry of Movement

The transit map is not a representation of geography. It is a representation of intention — the intention to move from one point to another with minimum cognitive friction. Every curve, every angle, every color choice is a decision about what the passenger needs to know and what they do not.

Consider the red line. In Zürich, it carries the heaviest traffic. In New York, it crosses Manhattan's spine. In Tokyo, it connects the imperial palace to the financial district. The color does not describe the route; it names it. It makes the abstract concrete.

"Clarity is not the absence of information. It is the presence of exactly the right information, in exactly the right form."

The Archive exists to preserve these decisions — not as artifacts of taste, but as documents of a particular way of thinking about public space, about the relationship between the individual and the system, between the body in motion and the map that guides it.

— Dr. Lena Wirth, Chief Curator, HTA

Current Exhibition
"Lines of Sight"

A seasonal presentation of forty works from the permanent collection, arranged chronologically to trace the evolution of transit information design from schematic diagram to digital interface. The exhibition includes original working drawings, production proofs, and installed signage elements.

Curated by Dr. Lena Wirth with contributions from the Swiss Design Center, Zürich.

Collection Introduction

An international museum collection devoted to public transportation maps, signs, tickets, clocks, and information systems. Assembled according to Swiss International Typographic principles: precision, clarity, and systematic design.

The archive documents how cities communicate movement. Every object is preserved as a study in legibility, material, and system.

Scope

Maps • Signage • Tickets • Clocks • Wayfinding • Type specimens • Color standards • Grid systems

Timeline of Information Design

  1. 1945
    Post-war rationalization. Grid systems introduced to signage.
  2. 1955
    Helvetica released. Adoption in transport typography begins.
  3. 1965
    Paris Métro standardizes pictograms and color coding.
  4. 1975
    Digital clocks and LED information boards deployed.
  5. 1990
    Unified wayfinding manuals across European networks.
  6. 2020
    Variable data and real-time systems integrated with archival standards.

Featured Designer Essay

Massimo Vignelli — Systems of Movement

Design for transit is design for the public. It must be legible at speed, durable in use, and neutral in expression. The system is the object.

“The map is not the territory. The sign is the contract between city and citizen.”

Consistency of type, line, and color creates trust. The archive preserves these contracts.

Essay details

First published 1972. Reprinted 2024 with permission. 1,200 words. Accompanying specimen sheet 12/72.
